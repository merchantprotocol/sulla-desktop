**When:** A human will read the output.

**Do:**
1. Lead with the actual point. Use concrete nouns and varied sentence length.
2. Use paragraphs for connected ideas and bullets only for truly discrete items.
3. Match the form to the job. Homepage copy needs a clear audience, promise, proof, objections, and call to action. A newsletter needs subject, preview, body, and one main CTA. A video script needs a truthful hook, timed beats, proof, and CTA. A meeting summary needs decisions, owners, dates, and open questions.
4. Preserve the human's voice and verified facts. Mark unknown names, figures, product claims, legal requirements, and dates instead of filling them in.
5. For policy or legal-facing prose, separate operational facts from recommendations and say that qualified review is needed before adoption. Do not present a draft as legal advice.
6. Read the final prose once for ambiguity, unsupported claims, accidental promises, private data, and missing next actions.
7. Pick a recommendation when comparing options and stop after the last substantive sentence.

**Check:** The intended reader, purpose, factual basis, and next action are clear. Every claim is verified or visibly marked for review.

**Don't:**
- add a closing recap, throat-clearing, or "hopefully this helps".
- overuse em dashes, corporate buzzwords, hedging, or rule-of-three lists.
- claim lived experience, invent testimonials or metrics, cite unnamed studies, or present a policy draft as adopted or legally sufficient.
